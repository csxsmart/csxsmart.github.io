Spatiotemporal Prediction & Reconstruction of Sparse Physical Fields

Multi-channel adaptive deep-learning architecture · Advisors: Prof. Jianwei You & Prof. Yiqian Mao (School of Information Science and Engineering, SEU)

A unified three-stage framework for predicting and reconstructing physical fields from heterogeneous, sparse sensor observations (geophysics, meteorology, aerospace) — where sources differ in channel count and spatiotemporal resolution.

  • Stage 1 — Multi-channel adaptive autoencoder: a shared trunk with channel-wise input/output adapter heads handles any number of channels; zero-init residual ResBlocks (GroupNorm / GELU) and dual-branch down/up-sampling keep deep training stable; a softclip2 latent saturation function suppresses latent blow-up.
  • Stage 2 — Latent temporal Transformer: a Transformer encoder with learnable positional encodings over latent sequences, using einops for efficient (B,T,C,H,W) ↔ (B,T,D) reshaping and configurable autoregressive multi-step forecasting.
  • Stage 3 — EDM-style diffusion refiner: a Diffusion UNet with sinusoidal time embeddings and multi-scale skips, using a 2nd-order Heun sampler to recover pixel-level detail in just 4–8 steps.

Independently designed and implemented the multi-channel adaptation modules and the latent diffusion-refinement pipeline; markedly improved reconstruction and multi-step prediction accuracy over baselines. Rated a Top 5% project.
